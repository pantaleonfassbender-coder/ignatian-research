Rights. The writings of Ignatius of Loyola (1491–1556) and of the early Society are long out of copyright; the modern translations are mostly not. This site ships four full texts: the book of the Spiritual Exercises in a public-domain parallel edition (Spanish Autograph and Latin Vulgata of 1548 from the Madrid 1919 Monumenta Ignatiana, with Elder Mullan's 1914 English translation); Letters and Instructions, vol. I, translated by D. F. O'Leary (1914), which is in the public domain; the Official Directory of 1599 in its original Latin from the same 1919 volume, accompanied by W. H. Longridge's public-domain English translation of 1919 and by an unofficial machine-generated working translation made for this site directly from that Latin; and the Memoriale of Blessed Peter Faber in the Latin of the editio princeps (Paris, 1873), likewise with an unofficial working translation. Longridge's 1919 commentary on the Exercises, with his Additional Notes, is included as a switchable layer of the trilingual reader. For the four in-copyright translations it ships no running text at all, but only derived data: canonical citation anchors, counts, registers and networks, together with editorial matter written for this site. Their full-text functions operate solely on a copy you load into your own browser, which is never uploaded.

Open the in-copyright works

Full-text functions for the four in-copyright translations run entirely in your browser. Drop in a PDF and this page will identify which work it is, read its text layer, and store it in your own device storage. Nothing is uploaded; only the passages you actively query in the dialogue leave your machine.

Status

Each work can also be opened from a named file directly, which overrides the recognition, and removed again if the wrong volume was filed under it.

Opening a work enables concordance, canonical citation of hits, and the evidence mode of the dialogue.
